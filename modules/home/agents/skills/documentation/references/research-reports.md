# Research Reports

Organize available research into a document that answers a question and makes the evidence assessable. This mode writes findings; a substantial new investigation needs a separately agreed scope rather than an implicit expansion of documentation work.

## Read First

Read the supplied question, notes, sources, and constraints. Check sources supporting consequential claims when accessible. Distinguish source statements, observations, inference, and recommendations. A source that was supplied but not inspected is not independently verified.

## Flexible Skeleton

1. **Question and Answer.** State the supported conclusion, with uncertainty where needed.
2. **Scope and Method.** Explain what was examined, relevant dates, and exclusions that affect the conclusion.
3. **Findings.** Group by the question's decision criteria; cite sources near the claims they support.
4. **Comparison or Recommendation.** Compare supported attributes. Label reasoning and tradeoffs as inference when they go beyond source statements.
5. **Limits and Open Questions.** Include conflicting evidence, missing measurements, stale information, and unresolved assumptions that could change the answer.

Prefer primary sources for technical facts. Preserve meaningful disagreements rather than forcing a single confident conclusion. Mark access limits and unsupported claims; citations cannot substitute for checking whether a source actually supports the text.

Include a retrieval or evidence date when timeliness affects the result. Keep quotations short and follow applicable source-use limits. Keep collected notes separate from the reader-facing synthesis when their detail would obscure the answer.
