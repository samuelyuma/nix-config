# Comparisons and Recommendations

Establish what decision the user is making and which criteria could change it: compatibility, workflow, cost, operating constraints, maintenance, or another stated goal. Ask only for consequential missing preferences. Research factual attributes yourself.

## Compare Fairly

- Use evidence under comparable versions, plans, platforms, dates, and measurement conditions. Separate advertised capabilities from independent observations and observed results from estimates.
- Apply hard requirements before softer preferences. An option that violates a required constraint is not made suitable by unrelated strengths.
- Use a table when several options share useful attributes. Keep unknown values explicit rather than treating them as zero, absent, or equivalent.
- Check current evidence for changing prices, availability, support, and product capabilities unless the user explicitly requests an historical or supplied-source comparison. State the scope and date when material.
- Use forums for attributed experiences or leads. Several anecdotes do not establish a benchmark, reliability rate, or general technical guarantee.

## Recommend

Recommend one option when the evidence and priorities support it. Explain the decisive reason and meaningful tradeoffs, tying them to the user's criteria. Label a recommendation as judgment rather than a source fact.

When priorities are unresolved, give a conditional comparison and ask the smallest useful round of questions. When evidence is insufficient, identify what would settle the decision; avoid a forced winner. A proposed experiment is not a completed measurement or an implementation commitment.
